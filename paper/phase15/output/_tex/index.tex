\begin{abstract}
We revisit American option pricing under Black--Scholes--Merton
assumptions with continuous dividend yield \(q\) through the one-driver
complex geometric Brownian motion of Phase 4, whose modulus is exactly
the stock price. In these coordinates the unknown exercise boundary
becomes a moving phase barrier. We first build the exact machinery: the
early-exercise-premium representation of Kim, Jacka, and
Carr--Jarrow--Myneni together with the nonlinear Volterra equation for
the boundary, solved by a stable causal level-set scheme that we
validate against published 10,000-step binomial benchmark values
(\(|\Delta|\le 2.2\times10^{-3}\)), McDonald--Schroder put--call
symmetry (\(\le 2.4\times10^{-3}\)), and Richardson-extrapolated trees.
The solver exposes two structural facts: the call residual vanishes
identically above the boundary (the boundary is where it \emph{reaches}
zero), and near maturity the boundary follows the infinite-slope shape
\(\sqrt{\tau|\ln\tau|}\) with an effective coefficient that depends on
\(r-q\). These facts motivate the Phase 15 pricing formula: a
\textbf{logarithmic-spiral boundary family}
\(\ln b(\tau)=\ln b_\infty+\ln(b_0/b_\infty)e^{-\kappa_1\tau}
+\eta\sigma\sqrt{2\tau|\ln\max(\tau,\varepsilon)|}\,e^{-\kappa_2\tau}\),
exact at both asymptotic limits --- the maturity anchor
\(b_0=K\max(1,r/q)\) (resp. \(K\min(1,r/q)\)) and the perpetual boundary
\(b_\infty\) --- with \((\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\eta)\) fixed by collocation
of the value-matching residual, no boundary fitting. The American price
is then European Black--Scholes plus one early-exercise-premium integral
over an explicit integrand. On a 90-configuration benchmark grid (calls
and puts, \(r,q\in\{0.03,\ldots,0.10\}\), \(\sigma\in\{0.2,0.4,0.6\}\),
\(T\in\{0.5,1,3\}\), five spots) the formula attains RMSE \(0.016\)
against the reference, versus \(0.285\) for Barone-Adesi--Whaley and
\(0.019\) for Richardson-extrapolated binomial trees, while remaining a
fast closed-form-class expression. We state precisely what is not
claimed: no closed form for the true boundary is asserted, and the
constant-coefficient free-boundary problem remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec-introduction}

The American option problem under Black--Scholes--Merton (BSM)
assumptions with a continuous dividend yield \(q\ge0\) has resisted
closed-form solution for half a century. Merton's observation that the
American call with \(q=0\) is worth exactly its European counterpart
(Merton 1973) is the exception that proves the rule: with \(q>0\) (or
for the put with any \(r>0\)) early exercise becomes optimal, and the
price is determined by a free boundary --- the critical stock price
\(b(t)\) below which (put) or above which (call) immediate exercise is
optimal. Kim derived the early-exercise-premium (EEP) representation and
showed that the boundary solves a nonlinear Volterra integral equation
(Kim 1990); Jacka and Carr--Jarrow--Myneni established the same
representation independently (Jacka 1991; Carr et al. 1992). Practical
pricing has since relied on trees, finite differences, Monte Carlo, and
analytic approximations --- most famously the quadratic approximation of
Barone-Adesi and Whaley (Barone-Adesi and Whaley 1987) and the
multipiece exponential boundary of Ju (Ju 1998).

This paper starts from a different object. The Phase 4 construction of
this research program (Complex--Itô Random-Walk Research Project 2026)
is the one-driver complex process

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-complex-gbm}{
\mathcal Z_t
=
\mathcal Z_0
\exp\!\left(
\left[\left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)+i\omega\right]t
+(\sigma+i\beta)W_t
\right),
}\end{equation}

whose modulus satisfies
\(d|\mathcal Z_t|/|\mathcal Z_t|=\mu\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t\): it is
\emph{exactly} geometric Brownian motion. Under the risk-neutral measure
with \(\mu=r-q\), the modulus is the BSM stock price \(S_t\), and the
phase lift is

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-phase}{
\Theta_t=\Theta_0+\omega t+\beta W_t
=\Theta_0+\frac{\beta}{\sigma}\ln\frac{S_t}{S_0}
+\left[\omega-\frac{\beta}{\sigma}\left(r-q-\frac12\sigma^2\right)\right]t.
}\end{equation}

In the drift-matched gauge (the bracket vanishes) the phase is a pure
scale of the log-price, and the American free-boundary problem becomes a
\textbf{moving phase-barrier problem}: the call exercise region
\(\{S_t\ge b(t)\}\) is the phase half-space
\(\{\Theta_t\ge\theta_B(t)\}\) with
\(\theta_B(t)=\Theta_0+(\beta/\sigma)\ln(b(t)/S_0)\). We use this
geometry to do three things.

\textbf{First (exact reference).} We implement the EEP representation
and the Volterra boundary equation as a stable \emph{causal level-set
solver} (Section~\ref{sec-reference}). Two structural facts organize the
numerics: for the call, the value-matching residual \(F(y;\tau)\)
satisfies \(F(y;\tau)<0\) for \(y<b(\tau)\) and \(F(y;\tau)=0\) for
\textbf{all} \(y\ge b(\tau)\) --- the residual \emph{reaches} zero
rather than crossing it (\textbf{?@sec-structure}) --- and the equation
loses traction as \(\tau\downarrow0\), where the maturity anchor takes
over. The reference prices reproduce published 10,000-step binomial
benchmark values and obey put--call symmetry
(Section~\ref{sec-validation}).

\textbf{Second (geometry of the boundary).} The reference solutions
expose the short-maturity law of the boundary: an infinite-slope
approach with the universal \emph{shape} \(\sqrt{\tau|\ln\tau|}\) and an
effective coefficient that depends on \(r-q\) at accessible maturities
(Section~\ref{sec-asymptotics}). No finite-slope ansatz (in particular,
no plain exponential spiral) can reproduce it.

\textbf{Third (the Phase 15 formula).} Guided by the phase picture, we
define the logarithmic-spiral boundary family, exact at \emph{both}
asymptotic limits --- the maturity anchor and the perpetual boundary ---
refined by the short-maturity asymptotic term
(Section~\ref{sec-spiral}). Its three remaining parameters are fixed by
collocation of the value-matching residual; no boundary fitting appears
anywhere. The resulting price --- European Black--Scholes plus one EEP
integral over an explicit integrand --- is a fast closed-form-class
formula. On a 90-configuration benchmark grid it beats
Barone-Adesi--Whaley by roughly an order of magnitude in RMSE and is on
par with, or better than, Richardson-extrapolated binomial trees
(Section~\ref{sec-results}).

\subsubsection{Novelty boundary}\label{sec-novelty}

\emph{Claimed as new:} the phase-barrier reformulation of the BSM
free-boundary problem on the Phase 4 complex GBM; the causal level-set
reference solver and its structural diagnostics (residual plateau,
traction loss, \(\sqrt{\tau|\ln\tau|}\) shape); the logarithmic-spiral
boundary family anchored exactly at both limits; the collocation-fixed
pricing formula and its benchmark performance. \emph{Not claimed:} the
EEP representation (Kim 1990; Jacka 1991; Carr et al. 1992), the
maturity anchors (Kim 1990), the perpetual closed form
(McDonald--Schroder, via (\textbf{merton1973-style?}) ODE roots),
quadratic or multipiece approximations (Barone-Adesi and Whaley 1987;
MacMillan 1986; Ju 1998), or any closed form for the true boundary ---
the constant-coefficient free-boundary problem remains open, and this
paper does not claim to solve it.

\section{Exact machinery}\label{sec-machinery}

\subsection{Risk-neutral dynamics and European
baseline}\label{risk-neutral-dynamics-and-european-baseline}

Under the risk-neutral measure \(\mathbb Q\),

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-dynamics}{
\frac{dS_t}{S_t}=(r-q)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t,
}\end{equation}

and the European price with dividend yield is the standard
Merton-adjusted Black--Scholes formula, which we write as
\(V_E(S,K,\tau;\phi)\) with \(\phi=+1\) for calls and \(\phi=-1\) for
puts. Put--call parity reads \(c-p=S e^{-q\tau}-K e^{-r\tau}\), and the
American call with \(q=0\) reduces exactly to the European call (Merton
1973).

\subsection{Early-exercise-premium representation}\label{sec-eep}

Let \(V\) be the American price and \(\tau=T-t\). Applying Itô's formula
to \(e^{-rt}V(S_t,t)\) and using that \(V\) solves the BSM PDE in the
continuation region and \(V=\phi(S-K)\) in the exercise region gives,
with \(B(u)\) the boundary in calendar time (Kim 1990; Jacka 1991; Carr
et al. 1992),

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-eep-call}{
V_A(S_t,t)=V_E(S_t,t)+\int_t^T e^{-r(u-t)}
\,\mathbb E_t^{\mathbb Q}\!\left[
\big(qS_u-rK\big)\,\mathbf 1_{\{S_u\ge B(u)\}}\right]du
}\end{equation}

for the call, with the integrand
\((rK-qS_u)\mathbf 1_{\{S_u\le B(u)\}}\) for the put. Evaluating the
conditional expectations in closed form yields the working
representation used throughout: with \(b(\tau)\) the boundary as a
function of time to maturity,

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-eep}{
\boxed{
V_A(S,\tau)=V_E(S,\tau)+\int_0^\tau
\Big[
qS e^{-qs}\Phi\!\big(\phi d_1(S,b(\tau-s),s)\big)
-rK e^{-rs}\Phi\!\big(\phi d_2(S,b(\tau-s),s)\big)
\Big]ds
}
}\end{equation}

for the call (\(\phi=+1\)); the put (\(\phi=-1\)) carries
\(\Phi(-d_1),\Phi(-d_2)\) with the two terms interchanged in sign, and

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-d12}{
d_{1,2}(S,B,s)=
\frac{\ln(S/B)+(r-q\pm\frac12\sigma^2)s}{\sigma\sqrt s}.
}\end{equation}

Three properties of Eq.~\ref{eq-eep} are essential for what follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Stationarity.} The price is stationary to first order under
  boundary perturbations (envelope property of the optimal stopping
  problem): a boundary approximation with small error produces a
  \emph{second-order} price error. This is why a coarse-boundary formula
  can price accurately.
\item
  \textbf{Causality.} The integral at time to maturity \(\tau\) involves
  the boundary only at arguments \(<\tau\). The boundary equation can be
  solved sequentially from \(\tau=0\) upward.
\item
  \textbf{Degeneracy above the boundary (call).} For \(y\ge b(\tau)\),
  immediate exercise is optimal, so the representation reproduces the
  intrinsic value identically:
  \((y-K)=V_E(y,\tau)+\mathrm{EEP}(y,\tau)\). Hence the value-matching
  residual \(F(y;\tau):=(y-K)-V_E(y,\tau)-\mathrm{EEP}(y;\tau)\)
  satisfies \(F<0\) for \(y<b(\tau)\) and \(F\equiv0\) for
  \(y\ge b(\tau)\): the boundary is where the residual \emph{reaches}
  zero. The put mirrors this with a positive hump and \(F\to-\infty\).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Boundary integral equation and anchors}\label{sec-anchors}

Evaluating Eq.~\ref{eq-eep} at the boundary (\(S=b(\tau)\), value
matching) gives the nonlinear Volterra equation

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-volterra}{
\phi\big(b(\tau)-K\big)
=
V_E(b(\tau),\tau)+
\int_0^\tau
\Big[
q\,b(\tau)e^{-qs}\Phi\big(\phi d_1(b(\tau),b(\tau-s),s)\big)
-rK e^{-rs}\Phi\big(\phi d_2(b(\tau),b(\tau-s),s)\big)
\Big]ds,
}\end{equation}

whose solution is the exercise boundary. The equation is anchored at
both ends of the maturity axis:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{At maturity} (Kim 1990; Jacka 1991): exercising the call at
  spot \(S\) just before expiry gains the dividend flow \(qS\) against
  the interest \(rK\) on the strike, so
  \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-anchors}{b_c(0^+)=K\max(1,r/q) \quad(q>0),\qquad
  b_p(0^+)=K\min(1,r/q),}\end{equation} with \(b_c(0^+)=\infty\) when
  \(q=0\) (never exercise);
\item
  \textbf{At infinity} the perpetual McDonald--Schroder closed form:
  with \(s_\pm\) the roots of \(\frac12\sigma^2 s(s-1)+(r-q)s-r=0\),
  \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-perpetual}{
  b_\infty^{\mathrm{call}}=K\frac{s_+}{s_+-1},\qquad
  b_\infty^{\mathrm{put}}=K\frac{s_-}{s_--1},
  }\end{equation} and the corresponding perpetual prices.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reference solver and validation}\label{sec-reference}

\subsection{Causal level-set scheme}\label{sec-solver}

We solve Eq.~\ref{eq-volterra} on a mildly clustered grid
\(\tau_i=T(i/n)^{3/2}\), sequentially in \(i\). At step \(i\) the
history \(b(\cdot)\) on \([0,\tau_{i-1}]\) is known; the residual
\(F(y;\tau_i)\) is evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature with history
interpolation, and \(b(\tau_i)\) is located as the solution of
\(F(y;\tau_i)=-\delta K\) (a genuine crossing, since \(F\) only
\emph{reaches} zero), corrected linearly back toward the zero level,
inside an adaptive scan window of width \(\sim8\sigma\sqrt{\tau_i}\)
around the previous boundary. The level \(\delta>0\) is needed because
exactly at the zero level the call problem is one-sided. The scheme
respects the degeneracies documented in Section~\ref{sec-eep}: the
\(\tau\downarrow0\) points, where \(\partial F/\partial y\to0\), are
pinned to the anchor Eq.~\ref{eq-anchors} rather than solved.

\section{Geometry of the boundary}\label{sec-asymptotics}

\subsection{Phase-barrier picture}\label{sec-phase-picture}

In the drift-matched gauge of Eq.~\ref{eq-phase}, the boundary is the
phase curve \(\theta_B(t)=\Theta_0+(\beta/\sigma)\ln(b(T-t)/S_0)\)
(Figure~\ref{fig-phase-boundary}): calls exercise above the curve, puts
below it, and both curves run from the maturity anchor to the horizontal
\emph{perpetual phase line} \(\theta_\infty\). The Phase 4 rank-one
structure (both radius and phase driven by one \(W\)) is precisely what
makes the barrier a \emph{curve} rather than a surface: the exercise
decision is a one-dimensional first-passage question in the phase
coordinate.

\subsection{Short-maturity shape}\label{sec-shape}

Fitting \(b(\tau)=b_0+\eta\,c\,\sigma b_0\sqrt{2\tau|\ln\tau|}\) to the

\item

\item
  The \textbf{coefficient} is near \(1\) only in the zero-drift case
  \(r=q\); nonzero \(r-q\) shifts it substantially (down to

  correction cannot be resolved on accessible windows; either way the
  practical coefficient is parameter-dependent.
\end{enumerate}

The second fact is a design input: the Phase 15 family keeps the shape
exactly and lets collocation fix the effective coefficient.

\section{The logarithmic-spiral boundary family}\label{sec-spiral}

\subsection{Definition and limits}\label{sec-limits}

Let \(b_0=b(0^+)\) be the maturity anchor Eq.~\ref{eq-anchors} and
\(b_\infty\) the perpetual boundary Eq.~\ref{eq-perpetual}. The
\textbf{refined spiral boundary} is

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-spiral}{
\boxed{
\ln b(\tau)
=
\ln b_\infty+\ln\!\left(\frac{b_0}{b_\infty}\right)e^{-\kappa_1\tau}
+\eta\,\sigma\sqrt{2\tau\,\big|\ln\max(\tau,\varepsilon)\big|}\,
e^{-\kappa_2\tau}
}
}\end{equation}

with \(\varepsilon\) a tiny cutoff, \(\eta>0\) for calls and \(\eta<0\)
for puts. The first two terms are the plain logarithmic spiral: in the
phase-time plane the boundary winds toward the perpetual phase line with
exponential rate \(\kappa_1\) --- the geometric object singled out by
Section~\ref{sec-phase-picture}. The third term adds the short-maturity
asymptotic shape with exponential cutoff \(\kappa_2\). By construction,

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-limits}{
b(0^+)=b_0,\qquad \lim_{\tau\to\infty}b(\tau)=b_\infty,
}\end{equation}

for all parameter values: the family is anchored exactly at both
asymptotic limits. Figure~\ref{fig-spiral-fit} compares best-fit members
against the reference boundary; the refined family reduces the relative
\(L^\infty\) boundary error by factors of \(2\)--\(10\) relative to the
plain spiral.

\subsection{Collocation}\label{sec-collocation}

The three parameters \((\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\eta)\) are fixed without any
boundary fitting: we drive the value-matching residual
Eq.~\ref{eq-volterra} of the spiral boundary itself to zero at three
fractional times to maturity \((\tau_1,\tau_2,\tau_3)=(T/4,T/2,3T/4)\),
by bounded least squares (multi-start L-BFGS-B, box
\(10^{-3}\le\kappa_i\le60\), \(0\le\eta\le2.5\)). The residual
evaluations reuse the exact EEP machinery of Eq.~\ref{eq-eep} with the
spiral boundary inside the integral, so the collocation is an honest
projection of the free-boundary equation onto the family --- no
reference solve is involved at pricing time.

\subsection{The pricing formula}\label{sec-formula}

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-formula}{
\boxed{
V_A(S,\tau)
=
V_E(S,\tau)
+
\int_0^\tau
\Big[
qS e^{-qs}\Phi\big(\phi d_1(S,b(\tau-s),s)\big)
-rK e^{-rs}\Phi\big(\phi d_2(S,b(\tau-s),s)\big)
\Big]ds
}
}\end{equation}

with \(b(\cdot)\) given by Eq.~\ref{eq-spiral} at the collocated
parameters (\(\phi=+1\) call; the put uses \(\Phi(-d_1),\Phi(-d_2)\)).
This is the \textbf{Phase 15 spiral formula}: European Black--Scholes
plus one one-dimensional integral over a fully explicit integrand. Its
limits are correct by construction: \(q=0\) calls return the European
price (the boundary is infinite, the premium vanishes); the boundary
limits Eq.~\ref{eq-limits} hold at both ends of the maturity axis; and
stationarity (Section~\ref{sec-eep}) insures the price against residual
boundary error to first order.

\section{Scope and limitations}\label{sec-scope}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Not a closed form.} We do not claim a closed-form American
  price. Eq.~\ref{eq-formula} is a closed-form-class expression (one
  bounded least-squares solve of three scalar equations, one smooth
  one-dimensional integral); the true free boundary remains open problem
  territory, and our own reference solver is a numerical scheme.
\item
  \textbf{Accuracy envelope.} On the extended grid the worst errors
  (\(\sim0.1\)--\(0.19\)) occur for long-maturity (\(T=3\)),
  high-volatility, dividend-heavy configurations. The formula is
  designed for \(T\lesssim 1\)--\(2\); beyond that, multipiece
  compositions of the family (piecewise collocation) or the reference
  solver are the appropriate tools.
\item
  \textbf{Stationarity is a shield, not a license.} Boundary errors of
  several percent can price to \(10^{-3}\) by stationarity, but a badly
  collocated family can still misprice; the benchmark grid is the
  operative guarantee.
\item
  \textbf{Short-maturity coefficient.} We document the coefficient's
  \(r-q\)-dependence as measured; the limiting constant(s) are not
  resolved. The \(\eta\) parameter makes the formula insensitive to this
  gap.
\item
  \textbf{Model scope.} Constant \(r,q,\sigma\); continuous dividends;
  standard single-asset American calls/puts. American options under the
  Phase 6 stochastic-correlation framework or with discrete dividends
  are open extensions.
\end{itemize}
